Project management — mockups

the dashboard already generates the raw material — tasks, sessions, tokens, handoffs, lineage, deadlines, heartbeats. these seven pages spend that data on managing the research. thrown at the wall on purpose; keep what sticks.
①

Mission control

The overview as a triage queue: every waiting question, stalled dependency and burn anomaly ranked into one list you clear from the top. Projects demote to context cards.
derivable today ②

The ledger

Cost accounting for research: the task-cost Pareto, spend by category, token mix by model tier. "Counterfactuals are $40 tasks; letters are $12 ones."
derivable today ③

The graph

Lineage drawn as a DAG per project, with the critical path to each deadline in red and an "unblocks the most" ranking. What blocks what, at a glance.
derivable today ④

The journal

The season on one page: task lifespans, artifacts ✦ and decisions ◇ stamped where they happened, waiting-on-you time as a visible shape, deadlines on the right. Plus the focused-hours heatmap.
derivable today ⑤

Momentum

Burnup with a forecast: does the current pace clear each deadline? The deadline-chain forecast (only the gating tasks) beside the naive one, and a thrash watch separating "hard" from "circling".
derivable · new heuristics ⑥

The registry

Numbers, decisions and artifacts collected out of handoffs into an institutional memory — every fact with its birth task, date and cost; superseded values struck through. Could feed the living abstract.
one new field: supersedes ⑦

The briefing

The dashboard writes your standup: a Friday wrap / Monday plan memo — prose with tappable numbers, the three questions waiting on you, the usage runway. Pushed via ntfy or read aloud.
numbers free · prose = 1 small call
Shared world. All pages read one fictional dataset (data.js — Alex Rivera's wedge-model / replication-kit / survey-pipeline, week of Jul 27 2026), so the ideas compare honestly. Series colors are the dashboard's purple/green/amber, validated for CVD + contrast in both themes (all-pairs); blue is reserved for chrome and sequential ramps.
